# Waypoint sequencing follow-ups #406, #407, #408: design

Date: 2026-10-27. Issues: #406, #407 and #408, all left over from the sequencing rework of
[2026-10-20-waypoint-sequencing-design.md](2026-10-20-waypoint-sequencing-design.md). Branch: `fix-406-407-408`.
A small change: three independent fixes in `NavCalculator` and `ActiveWaypoint`.

## 1. Intent

Bring three details of the turn and sequencing behavior in line with the KLN 89 trainer, which was checked for them on
2026-10-27 (`docs/kln89-trainer-data/2026-10-27/`, `report.md` and `report-2.md`, runs a1 to a16, plus a re-read of
the 2026-10-20 recordings). The goal of the project is faithful emulation (CLAUDE.md), so each rule below comes from
the trainer, or is marked as a call where the trainer did not settle it.

**Non-goals.** Two other differences that the check found are filed separately and are not touched here: the 2.8 NM
gate of the turn plan looks lower at 119 kt in flight (#438), and on the abeam plan the trainer sometimes starts the
normal turn circle late instead of the zero-size abeam turn (#439). Their constants and code paths stay as they are.

**Anti-goals.** A second set of values for the SimVars beside the displayed ones (the maintainer's rule of 2026-10-20).
Any change of name or meaning in the public contract.

## 2. Behavior

### 2.1 #407: no lag after a turn start or a sequencing

In the trainer, at every turn start and sequencing, the new ident, distance, bearing, ETE, DTK, TO/FROM and XTK all
appear in the same display update, and so does the message prompt of a course change. This was checked on every
recorded sequencing and turn start of 2026-10-20 and 2026-10-27, high confidence. The XTK line can come up to 0.5 s
earlier; that is the trainer drawing its rows at different moments within one update.

The unit therefore shows the values of the state *after* the tick's turn and sequencing decisions, in the same
calculation. This covers NAV 1, NAV 3 and everything `setOutput` writes. The course messages of #434 also post in the
calculation of the leg change, not one later.

### 2.2 #406: the end of the alert after an abeam turn

The trainer ends the waypoint alert when the aircraft passes the far end of the turn it is flying. High confidence:
eight abeam passes and more than twenty turn-plan passes.

- **Turn circle:** the far end is the lead L past the waypoint on the next leg. The current end of the turn circle
  (`isPastEndOfTurn`) already matches this, so normal turns are unchanged.
- **Abeam turn:** the far end is the abeam line of the old waypoint on the new leg, that is, the along-track position
  past the waypoint on the new leg reaching 0. The unit ends the alert there, not at the sequencing.
- **What does not end it:** the track angle to the new DTK, a cross-track value, or a time. The "within about 5°" of
  the 2026-10-20 design (section 3.5) and of the #406 issue text was a coincidence of uncapped turns. An aircraft that
  does not progress along the new leg keeps the alert on: about 3 minutes at 10 kt in a1.
- **Between the sequencing and that line:** XTK and DTK are those of the new leg. That is what the trainer's line 2
  showed in R6, and it is what the unit shows today.
- **My call (not flown in the trainer):** more than 4.0 NM from the new leg also ends it, as it ends a turn circle
  (the 2026-10-20 design, section 3.2).

### 2.3 #408: an edit of the leg after the active waypoint

When the leg after the active waypoint changes while the active leg stays the same, the unit computes the turn again
for the new geometry. The edit can be an insert after the active waypoint, a delete of the next waypoint, or a
procedure load that replaces the next leg. Measured in the trainer:

- **Before the turn starts, on the turn plan (a3, a4, a6):** the old lead does nothing and the turn starts at the new
  lead, at once when the aircraft is already inside it. When the new turn point is more than about 20 s away, the
  alert goes off at the edit and comes back about 20 s before it.
- **After the turn starts (a2, a5):** the DTK goes back to the inbound leg and a new turn starts at the new lead.
  The waypoint is sequenced at the new turn's bisector, and the alert ends at the new turn's end. An aircraft already
  past the new bisector is sequenced at once (a11).
- **The active waypoint becomes the last one (a9, one run):** the turn is cancelled. The waypoint then gets the alert
  of a waypoint without a turn and is not sequenced.
- **An edit further down the plan (a7):** changes nothing.
- **On the abeam plan (a8, a16; medium-high confidence):** the plan is kept. There was no turn at the new lead, even
  with the aircraft inside it and only 1.0 to 1.7 NM off; the unit waited for the abeam line.

**The rule (the maintainer's choice of rule (c), 2026-10-27).** On a change of the following leg:

- drop the waypoint's own started turn (`ownTurnStarted` and what it put on the stack);
- keep an `AT_ABEAM` plan;
- clear an `AT_LEAD` plan, so that it is decided again by the normal rule: at the decision point of the new geometry,
  or at once when the aircraft is already past it.

A turn still on the stack from the previous waypoint is not the active waypoint's and stays. An abeam turn that had
already started is dropped too. It starts again in the next calculation onto the new next leg, because the plan is
still `AT_ABEAM` and the aircraft is FROM.

The trainers could not tell rule (c) from the alternative "decide again at the edit with a tighter gate". The two
differ only for a turn-plan pass edited 0.6 to about 1.8 NM off course (`report-2.md`).

## 3. Approaches

### #407

- **Chosen: recompute after the decisions.** The block that computes XTK, DTK, TO/FROM, distance, bearing and ETE
  becomes a method. It runs at the start of the tick, as now, because the decisions read TO/FROM and the ETE. It runs
  again when the tick changed the turn stack or the active leg.
  - The DTK difference of a leg change (`legChangeDtkDelta`) is then taken within the one tick: the DTK of the first
    pass against the DTK of the second.
  - The deferral of #434 goes: `NavPageState.legChangePending` and `legChangeFrom`, with their reset in
    `VolatileMemory`.
  - The second pass runs only in a tick where something changed, so the tick cost barely changes.
- **Rejected: split the tick into a decision part and an output part.** It is cleaner on paper but rearranges a file
  that took several rounds to get right. The decisions would still need a TO/FROM taken from the old leg, so it
  converges on the chosen approach.

### #406

- **Chosen: a run-out entry on the turn stack.** At the sequencing, the abeam turn on the stack becomes a run-out
  entry:
  - its path and DTK path are the new active leg;
  - its switch point is the old waypoint;
  - it ends once the aircraft is past the abeam line of that point on the new leg, or more than 4.0 NM from the leg.

  The existing alert paths already keep the alert on while any entry is on the stack (#156), both for a waypoint with
  a turn and for one without, including off the leg. Roll steering steers on the leg path, a great circle, which is
  the new leg. The next waypoint's own turn replaces the stack as it already does (`startOwnTurn`).
- **Rejected: a separate flag "alert until abeam of P".** Every alert path would have to read it, and it needs its own
  reset.

### #408

- **Chosen: a following-leg instance check.** `ActiveWaypoint` remembers the instance of `legs[fplIdx + 1]` (none for
  the last leg). It compares it in `assertToMatchesFplIdx`, in the same way and at the same moments as it already
  compares the active leg instance. When the instance changed, it applies the rule of 2.3 and remembers the new one.
  - Inserts, deletes and procedure loads are all caught.
  - An edit further down keeps the instance, so it changes nothing.
  - A Direct To to an FPL 0 waypoint is covered the same way.
- **Rejected: subscribe to flight-plan change events.** That means more moving parts, while the lazy check already
  sees every case before any calculation reads the leg.

## 4. Constraints

- **Public contract:** no H event, LVar, panel.xml key or SimVar changes name or meaning. The sequencing outputs move
  one second earlier, which is the point of #407.
- **State:** new turn state lives on `ActiveWaypoint` or `NavPageState`. Every power-on clears it (`VolatileMemory`
  reset; the turn state already goes through `clearTurnStack`). The remembered following-leg instance is a reference,
  not turn state. Whether and where to reset it is left to the builder, as long as a power-on cannot misread it as an
  edit or miss a real one.
- **Tick cost:** the recompute pass and the run-out end are a few geo operations, in change ticks only.
  `target: es2017`.
- **Commits:** one `fixes #NN` commit per issue, each with its tests and doc changes, in the order #407, #406, #408. The
  trainer data with its README and reports, and a 2026-10-27 block in `docs/kln89-trainer-record.md`, go in their own
  commit before them (a plain sentence that references the three issues). This design goes in with the data commit.
- **Comments:** each rule cites the trainer run that settled it ("checked in the KLN 89 trainer, 2026-10-27, run aN"),
  or says that it is a call. `docs/architecture.md` (the turn state around line 497, the course messages around line
  987) follows the code.

## 5. Tests

Expectations come from geometry and the trainer record, never from the code under test (test/README.md). Each fix
gets a test at the cheapest stage that observes it, most of them flight tests.

- **#407:**
  - In the calculation where the turn goes onto the stack, `desiredTrack` and the GPS DTK SimVar are the next leg's.
  - In the calculation of a sequencing, the distance, XTK and TO/FROM refer to the new waypoint, both at the bisector
    and without turn anticipation.
  - `turnLead.test.ts` and `bisectorSequencing.test.ts` drop their one-second allowance.
  - The leg-change tests of `NavCalculator.test.ts` and `PersistentMessages.test.ts` follow the same-tick delta, and a
    course message posts in the calculation of the turn start.
- **#406:** on the abeam flight of `offCourseSequencing.test.ts`:
  - the alert is still on in the calculations after the sequencing;
  - it is off once the aircraft is past the old waypoint's abeam line on the new leg, within one tick of flight;
  - XTK in between is the new leg's.

  The turn-plan alert end stays as it is.
- **#408:** the issue's reproduction (the standard route plus a fourth waypoint, an `insertLeg` after ABC):
  - **Insert before the turn:** the turn starts at the new lead.
  - **Insert during the turn:** the DTK goes back to the inbound course, then the turn starts at the new lead, and ABC
    is sequenced at the new bisector.
  - **Delete down to the last waypoint:** there is no turn, and there is the no-turn alert.
  - **Edit after the next waypoint:** nothing changes.
  - **Abeam plan, then an insert inside the new lead:** no turn until the abeam line, then sequencing at the new
    bisector.
- Every commit runs `npm test`, `npx tsc --noEmit` and `npm run build`.
